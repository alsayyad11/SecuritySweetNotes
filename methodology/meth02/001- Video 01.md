## How to Find Bugs in Small Scope Programs Using Out-of-Scope Information

**Speaker:** Full-time Bug Bounty Hunter from Jordan
**Platform:** HackerOne & Bugcrowd
**Context:** This is the first part of a full methodology series. The speaker chose to record a video instead of writing a write-up because the amount of detail and real examples would be too much to write.

---

##  Objective of Part 1

> How to find valuable bugs (including Critical ones) in programs that have very **limited scope**, like one or two subdomains only.

He said clearly:

> *“I don’t think outside the box... I think outside the house.”*

This means he doesn’t limit his recon to what’s written in the scope. He explores everything **around** the target — related domains, leaked credentials, JS files, etc.
But most importantly:
**He never attacks anything that’s out-of-scope — he just collects useful intel from it** and uses that to find bugs **inside the allowed scope**.

---

##  Step-by-Step Methodology (As Explained in the Video)

---

### ① When he sees a program with very limited scope

Let’s say the scope only includes:

* `vpn.underarmour.com`
* `login.underarmour.com`

Most hunters would just do basic scans (port scan, dirsearch, etc.) on those two subdomains.

But instead, he goes further.

---

### ② He looks for **related domains and infrastructure**

He asks:

> What other domains or services are related to this company?

Examples:

* `underarmour.net`
* `uat.underarmour.com`
* `cdn.underarmour.io`
* `ua.com`
* `uacdn.com`

He collects these domains — even if they are not in scope — **but doesn’t attack them**.
He uses them to search for leaks or mistakes.

---

### ③ Searches GitHub for sensitive data

He uses **GitHub Dorks** like:

```
"password" "underarmour.com"
"secret" "underarmour"
site:github.com underarmour
```

He looks for:

* Employee emails & passwords
* API keys
* Access tokens
* `.env` files
* Exposed JS with sensitive content

---

### ④ Tries credentials on login pages that are in scope

Let’s say he found:

* Email: `john@underarmour.com`
* Password: `Winter2023!`

He tests them on:

* `vpn.underarmour.com`
* `login.underarmour.com`

If login is successful → That’s a **Critical bug**:

* Broken Authentication
* Credential Stuffing
* Unauthorized Access

And it’s fully legal because the **exploit happened inside scope**, even if the intel came from outside.

##  Real-World Examples From the Video

---

###  Example 1: Leaked Credentials → Login Success

* He found leaked credentials (username/password) from a domain that’s not in scope.
* He tried them on a scoped login page.
* Login was successful.
* He submitted the report → marked as Critical.

---

###  Example 2: Signup as Employee

* He found an out-of-scope domain that allowed signup.
* He used his personal email (Gmail/Yahoo).
* Signup was successful, and he gained access to a system.
* That system had integration with scoped assets.
* Bug accepted → Report marked as Critical.

---

###  Example 3 (From his friend HackerX007):

* His friend analyzed JavaScript files.
* Found a hidden domain (not in scope) and an **access token** in the JS file.
* The JS file also explained how to use the token.
* The token provided access to an **Admin panel** with full privileges.
* Submitted → Accepted → Bounty: **\$15,000**

##  Golden Rule He Repeats

> **No one said “don’t look outside the scope” — but don’t attack outside the scope.**

You are allowed to **observe, collect information, and analyze** anything.
But all **testing and exploitation must happen inside the approved scope**.

---

##  Practical Checklist – Summary of His Workflow

| Step | Action                                                    |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Read the official scope carefully                         |
| 2    | Identify related domains and infrastructure               |
| 3    | Search GitHub, Google, Shodan for leaks                   |
| 4    | If you find credentials → test them **inside scope only** |
| 5    | Read JavaScript files carefully                           |
| 6    | Correlate all findings, then report any valid issue       |

| GitHub Dorks    | For hunting secrets (emails, keys, tokens)   |

---

## Takeaway

* **Out-of-scope ≠ useless**
* You can use out-of-scope assets as a **source of information**, not as targets
* Many Critical bugs start with a leak or clue that lives **outside** the visible scope
* It's all about smart, responsible recon
